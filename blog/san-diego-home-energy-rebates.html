San Diego Home Rebates in 2026: SDG&E Offers and the $2/sq ft Landscape Program

Updated July 2026 · 6 min read

San Diego's rebate landscape in 2026 looks different from Sacramento's or San Jose's. The utility-side offers are more modest — but San Diego has something the inland metros don't push nearly as hard: serious money for what's in your yard. Here's the honest picture.

SDG&E: smaller rebates, wider catalog

SDG&E's residential offers in 2026 run through instant and mail-in rebates on qualifying products — smart thermostats (up to ~$75), efficient water heaters, room ACs, and a rotating catalog of products in the roughly $20–$500 range. There are also income-qualified programs (ESA, CARE) that provide free weatherization and bill discounts for eligible households.

Individually small — but they layer on top of the bigger decision: making an older home more efficient in the first place. San Diego's coastal-canyon housing stock is full of 1960s–1980s homes with original insulation and aging HVAC, and those fundamentals are where monthly bills are actually won or lost.

The big one: $2 per square foot for replacing your lawn

Through the SoCal Water$mart program (funded by the Metropolitan Water District), San Diego–area homeowners may qualify for $2 per square foot — up to 5,000 sq ft per year — for removing grass and replacing it with approved water-wise landscaping. On a typical 1,000 sq ft front lawn, that's a potential $2,000, plus $100 per tree planted (up to 5 trees).

Requirements include a minimum 250 sq ft project, at least 3 plants per 100 sq ft, and permeable surfaces throughout the converted area.

The honest part: artificial turf does NOT qualify

Read this before anyone sells you turf "with a rebate": the SoCal Water$mart program explicitly excludes synthetic turf — "No synthetic turf is allowed" is written directly in the eligibility rules. If a salesperson promises you the $2/sq ft rebate on an artificial turf installation, they are wrong, and you'd be the one left without the rebate.

Does that mean artificial turf is a bad idea? Not at all — it stands on its own merits: no watering, no mowing, green year-round. Many San Diego homeowners do both: a rebate-qualifying water-wise conversion on the front yard, and artificial turf for the kids' play area or dog run in the back — applying the rebate only to the qualifying portion. The point is to make the decision with accurate information.

Permeable pavers: the quiet fit

Here's a detail most homeowners miss: the turf program requires converted areas to be permeable to air and water. Properly installed permeable hardscape — stepping stones, flagstone with sand joints — can be part of a qualifying design, while poured concrete disqualifies the area. If you're already thinking about a patio or walkway, designing it with the program rules instead of against them is free money left on the table otherwise.

What a smart San Diego upgrade sequence looks like

  1. Assess the envelope first — insulation, windows, HVAC age. That's where monthly bills live.
  2. Check current program status — water rebate funding varies by agency and can pause; verify before committing.
  3. Design the yard around the rules — qualifying plants and permeable surfaces where the rebate applies; turf where it makes lifestyle sense.
