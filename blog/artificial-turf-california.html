Artificial Turf in California: Save Water, Cut Maintenance (and the Rebate Truth)

Updated July 2026 · 6 min read

Between drought years, rising water bills, and endless mowing, a lot of California homeowners are done fighting to keep a green lawn alive. Artificial turf promises the opposite: green all year, no watering, no mowing. It's a genuinely good option for many yards — but there's one myth you need to clear up before anyone sells it to you.

The real benefits of artificial turf

  • Water savings — a natural lawn can be one of the biggest water users on your property; turf eliminates that
  • Zero mowing and maintenance — no mowing, fertilizing, or reseeding
  • Green all year — it doesn't go brown in summer heat or drought restrictions
  • Great for play areas and pets — durable and low-mess

The rebate truth — read this before you buy: California water agencies offer generous "turf replacement" rebates (often around $2 per square foot), but they explicitly exclude synthetic/artificial turf. If a salesperson promises you a water rebate on fake grass, they're wrong — and you'd be the one left without it.

So how do smart homeowners handle it?

You don't have to choose. Many California homeowners do both:

  • Front yard: a water-wise landscape conversion (drought-friendly plants + permeable surfaces) that qualifies for the rebate
  • Back yard: artificial turf for the kids' play area or dog run, where low-maintenance matters most

You apply the rebate only to the qualifying portion, and enjoy turf where it makes life easier. The key is designing the project with the rules in mind — not against them.

Bottom line: artificial turf stands on its own merits — water savings, no maintenance, year-round green. Just don't buy it expecting a water rebate. A free assessment can help you plan a yard that captures rebates where they apply and uses turf where it fits.

Frequently asked questions

Does artificial turf qualify for the water rebate?

No — water-agency turf-replacement rebates exclude synthetic turf. The rebate is for living water-wise plants and permeable surfaces. Don't let anyone tell you otherwise.

Is artificial turf still a good choice?

Yes, on its own merits: no watering, no mowing, green all year, and great for kids and pets. Just budget for it without expecting a water rebate.

Can I do both turf and a rebate landscape?

Absolutely — a rebate-qualifying water-wise conversion in front, turf in back. A contractor can design it so you capture the rebate where it applies.
