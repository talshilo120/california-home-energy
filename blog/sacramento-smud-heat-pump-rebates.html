SMUD Heat Pump Rebates in 2026: What Sacramento Homeowners Need to Know

Updated July 2026 · 6 min read

Here's a piece of good news that most Sacramento homeowners haven't heard yet: while the federal energy tax credits expired at the end of 2025, SMUD went the other direction — and raised its rebates for 2026.

If your home still runs on an aging gas furnace or gas water heater, this may be one of the better windows Sacramento has seen to switch to efficient electric heat pump systems. Here's what's on the table and what to watch for.

What SMUD is offering in 2026

UpgradeRebate (as reported by SMUD)
Gas-to-electric heat pump HVAC conversionUp to $3,000
Electric-to-electric heat pump HVAC upgrade$1,000
Gas-to-electric heat pump water heaterUp to $4,000
Electric-to-electric heat pump water heater$1,000

Amounts are as published by SMUD for 2026 and are subject to program terms, funding availability, and change without notice. Qualifying HVAC systems must meet efficiency requirements (two-stage 15.2 SEER2 minimum or variable-stage).

Why a heat pump, anyway?

A heat pump replaces both your air conditioner and your furnace with a single electric system that moves heat instead of generating it — cooling in Sacramento's brutal summers, heating in winter. Modern systems are dramatically more efficient than the equipment installed in most Sacramento-area homes 15–20 years ago, which is exactly why SMUD is willing to help pay for the switch.

For a home with an aging gas furnace and AC, the practical case looks like this:

  • You're likely to replace the old system soon anyway — most HVAC systems last 15–20 years
  • One modern system replaces two old ones
  • The rebate lands at the moment of purchase, when it matters most
  • A more efficient system means a structurally lower energy load, every month after

The catch you need to know about

SMUD rebates require a participating contractor. The system must be installed by a contractor enrolled in the SMUD Contractor Network — buy the same equipment from an unenrolled installer and the rebate may simply not apply. Before signing with anyone, confirm they can process the SMUD rebate for you.

Can rebates be stacked?

SMUD notes that its heat pump rebates can be stacked with other statewide and federal incentive programs where available. In practice, in mid-2026, the statewide HEEHRA single-family rebates are fully reserved with a waitlist — so what's actually stackable changes month to month. This is exactly the kind of thing a knowledgeable contractor checks for you at quote time.

Is your home a good candidate?

Signals that a heat pump conversion is worth investigating for your Sacramento home:

  • Your furnace or AC is 12+ years old
  • Your summer electric bills are painful even with conservative thermostat settings
  • You're on natural gas and watching gas rates climb
  • Some rooms never quite cool down or warm up
